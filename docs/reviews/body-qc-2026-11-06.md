## Scope and method

- Reviewed all 100 frames: Human and Elf, idle (4 frames) and walk (6 frames), 5 directions. Frame numbers are 0-based sheet indices.
- Dwarf was not reviewed: `art/bodies/dwarf/sheet.json` did not exist at the start, and production was still on Dwarf walk NW when this was written.
- Every frame was viewed with its raw pair beside the cell. Per-frame metrics compared the cells with the rig guide silhouettes and `joints.json`.
- Re-running `main_figure` → `to_cells` → `clean_alpha` in memory matches both sheets exactly (0 alpha pixels differ on all 100 frames). The sheets are current, so everything below applies to the present code.
- Candidate fixes were tried in memory only. Nothing in `pipeline/`, `art/` or the SD webui was touched.
- **Not checked:** the frames were not played in the animation lab or `qc.html`, so flicker findings come from comparing stills. The hand-written rows are visual judgements. Only the E2 rows and the numbers quoted in the table are measured.

## Extraction root causes

### E1: fragment of a second head kept (Human walk S f3, f5)

This is the fragment in the user's screenshot. Lab "frame 4/6" with the closed-eyes face is f3.

**The fragment was already connected in the raw mask. The 10px blocking step did not merge it.**

1. The old tags made the model draw a third figure across the middle of the 1216 px image. `halves()` cuts it at x=608, so its right part lands at the left edge of the right half.
2. That head touches the main head in the raw pixels, around x=148. The contact runs y≈328–487 in f3 and y≈408–439 in f5 (half coordinates).
3. `figure_mask` therefore sees one object, and `main_figure` (`bodies.py:74`) keeps it as the largest component. The main component grows to 266,250 and 262,877 px, against about 215,000 for a clean S frame.
4. `to_cells` copies it faithfully. The half's left edge maps to cell x≈33, which gives the straight vertical cut.
5. `frame_qc` sees one island, so nothing is reported. `cell_metrics` measures only top, bottom and islands, never width.

Stray pieces that do not touch the figure are dropped correctly. The third head in Human idle S f2/f3 is an example.

**Proposed change (not applied):**

- In `qc_pair` (`bodies.py:98`), after `fig = main_figure(half)`, add an issue when `fig[:, 0].any() or fig[:, -1].any()`. Do the same in `frame_qc`. On these 100 frames it fires on exactly f3 and f5, with no false positives.
- Do not rely on cropping these frames. Cutting the mask to the guide silhouette dilated by 64 px removes about 33,000 of the roughly 50,000 extra px, but leaves a slab of head attached. The contact in f3 is also about 160 px tall, too long for a morphological opening to separate cleanly. These frames need a redo.

### E2: enclosed background kept as opaque white

`figure_mask` (`sheet.py:135`) flood-fills background only from the image border. White enclosed by limbs and torso therefore counts as figure, and it shows as white on the game map. The rig contributes: the guide has a triangular gap between arm and waist on back and front views, and the model leaves that gap white and closes it with the arm and shorts.

- The pockets are true background: their mean colour is about (253, 253, 253).
- 63 of 100 frames lose at least one cell if pockets are cleared; 36 lose 8 or more and have rows above.
- Worst cases: Elf idle NW and idle N f0/f1 (46–48 cells), Human idle N f0/f1 and walk NW f5 (33 cells).
- The Elf idle NW leg sliver is one pocket of about 3,000 px.

**Proposed change (not applied):** in `main_figure`, or in a new step used only by `cmd_extract` (`figure_mask` is shared with paperdoll2), remove connected regions that meet all of these:

- pure white, `a.min(axis=2) >= 245`
- area ≥ 64 px
- below the neck, `y > FEET_Y + (head_y + 22 - 122) * 10`

Use 245, not the mask's 215 threshold: the test at 215 also caught shirt highlights (mean about 220) on Elf S frames. The neck limit protects eye whites and head highlights. The counts above come from the 215 version, so re-check them after changing the threshold.

### E3: broken, speckled outlines

The Elin LoCon draws on an 8 px grid (edge energy peaks at phase 7 of 8 in x and y on Human walk S, and mask extents step by 8). `to_cells` samples on a 10 px grid, so blocks straddle art pixels and clean 1-pixel outlines turn into dashes and bumps. This grid mismatch is the likely cause; it was not proven by a working fix.

Raw and cell compared at zoom: Elf idle N f3 right leg, Elf idle S f1 right leg, Human walk SW f2 legs. The raw outlines are clean in all three.

**Tested:** letting dark pixels win a block at ≥ 24% coverage did not visibly help.

**Untested options:** sample the art on its own 8 px grid first (1216 → 152 art px), then reduce with an outline-aware rule; or change the scale so the two grids agree. The second affects the spec's 1 game px = 10 generation px, so it is a design decision.

### E4: eye colour lost

52 of 54 S/SW/W frames with blue eyes in the raw lose more than half of their blue strength in the cell. Example: Human idle W f0 is vivid blue raw and grey-white in the cell.

The eyes are only a few art pixels, so the majority vote probably lets white highlights and lashes win. The 48-colour palette also comes from the whole half, background included. Fitting the palette to figure pixels only gave a small gain (Human walk SW f0: 78 → 94; Human idle W f0: no change).

**Proposed direction:** fit the palette to figure pixels (cheap, small gain). A full eye fix is still open; the spec's later `face` layer may be the better place for it.

### QC gaps

- `frame_qc` flagged 1 of the 72 faulty frames. It has no check for width, side extent, ears, outfit or pockets.
- `skin_lab` reports no drift on Elf W, even with the torso bare.
- `generate()` saves the last attempt even if all 3 fail QC (`bodies.py:128`), and `cmd_extract` never checks. Current `qc_pair` fails 8 Human pairs and no Elf pairs.
- Cheap additions: half-edge touch (E1), pure-white pocket area (E2), shirt present (grey vs skin share of the torso box), and ear extent per direction. In the torso-box metric, the 4 shirtless Elf W frames read 0 grey cells; all other Elf W frames read 93 or more.

## Generation and rig root causes

### R1: Elf ears are not in the rig

`rig.py:168` gives every body the same small ear, `V(1.0, 2.0, 1.4)`, and `Body` has no ear setting. The Elf guide shows only a small nub. Where depth is strong (N and NW at 0.8, SW at 0.75) the guide wins over `long pointy ears`. S is looser (0.55), so ears appear, but their length is unstable.

Extraction is not removing ears. The largest non-white area outside the mask on any Elf frame is 116 px, which is too small to be an ear, and the raw and cell ear outlines agree in the frames viewed.

| direction | head band in cells (head is about 47–80) | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| S idle | 38–89 | good and stable |
| S walk | f0/f1 38–87, f2/f3 46–81, f4/f5 37–90 | unstable |
| SW | idle 49–80; walk from 47–78 up to 44–85 | missing or popping |
| W | ear inside the head outline | acceptable, shape varies |
| NW | idle 49–80; walk from 47–78 up to 41–85 | missing or popping |
| N | 46–81 | stubs only |

**Proposed change (not applied):** add an ear length to `Body` (default: the present ear) and give Elf a long ear that reaches about 9 cells past the head, like the good S frames. Re-run `rig.py guides elf`, then redo the affected Elf pairs. Do this after production ends, because the run has `rig.py` loaded. Whether a stronger guide alone fixes the ears is untested; try it on one N pair first.

### R2: hand over the crotch in SW walk

The Elf guide `walk_SW_1` places the hand there, and the same pose appears on Human and Elf. A redo with the same guide is unlikely to change it.

### R3: Elf S shirt tone alternates by half

Across all 5 Elf S pairs the left frame's shirt is dark (median red channel about 170) and the right is light (about 210). The difference is in the raw pairs and is much larger than on other directions. Because it follows the half, a new seed may not fix it. Try one redo first; if it persists, normalise the shirt colours to a reference frame after extraction.

### Human: all 25 pairs use the old prompt

The task notes describe old tags on Human S walk, but the frame JSON files show `multiple views` in all 25 Human pairs. All 25 Elf pairs use the new tags. The old tags are the likely source of the extra heads and mini figures in Human raws. Most of those are dropped safely, so not every pair needs a redo for that reason alone. Regenerating all of Human would give it the same prompt as the other bodies.
